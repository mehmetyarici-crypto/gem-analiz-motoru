> فَإِذَا ٱنسَلَخَ ٱلْأَشْهُرُ ٱلْحُرُمُ فَٱقْتُلُوا۟ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ حَيْثُ وَجَدتُّمُوهُمْ وَخُذُوهُمْ وَٱحْصُرُوهُمْ وَٱقْعُدُوا۟ لَهُمْ كُلَّ مَرْصَدٍۢ ۚ فَإِن تَابُوا۟ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَوُا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ فَخَلُّوا۟ سَبِيلَهُمْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ

**Literal Analysis and Dual Reading (Section 4.1-E):**

- **Zamakhsharī's Analysis (*al-Kashshāf*):** The *alif-lām* in the word `ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ` is for *ʿahd* (specific reference), not for *istiġrāq* (universality). The address here is specific to the Meccan polytheists and their allies who unilaterally violated the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyya and other peace agreements, struck from behind, and waged active war against the Muslims. Zamakhsharī states that the metaphor of `ٱنسَلَخَ` (stripping out) emphasizes the definitive expiration of the deadline, and that the commands that follow are not arbitrary violence but stages of strategic blockade and deterrent military operation (*aḥz*, *ḥaṣr*, *marṣad*).
- **Dual Reading Note:**
  - **Reading 1 (Extremist / Literalist School — Muqātil b. Sulaymān, Ibn Ḥazm):** This verse has abrogated (*naskh*) the previous 120+ verses of peace, pardon, and tolerance; war against all polytheists on earth is fundamental until they believe.
  - **Reading 2 (Uṣūlī / Verification School — Ṭabarī, Jassāṣ, Rāzī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Shāṭibī):** There is no *naskh* in the verse; there is *takhṣīṣ* (specification) and *takyīd* (restriction). The ruling is directed at the warrior enemy who violates his covenant and makes aggression a habit.
  - **Preferred:** Reading 2. **Reason:** As Shāṭibī and Ibn ʿĀshūr have demonstrated, the Qurʾān's firmly established principles of justice and fidelity to covenant (al-Mumtaḥina 60:8-9, al-Baqara 2:190) cannot be abrogated; the claim of *naskh* is a methodological weakness that ignores context.

### STEP 01 — QURANIC TEXT → CONTEXT → CLASSICAL EXEGESIS (Mandatory + Section 4.1 Dual Reading)
* **Text (Nass):**
  > وَٱلسَّارِقُ وَٱلسَّارِقَةُ فَٱقْطَعُوٓا۟ أَيْدِيَهُمَا جَزَآءًۢ بِمَا كَسَبَا نَكَـٰلًۭا مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌۭ
  *(As for the male and female thief, amputate their hands as recompense for what they committed, as an exemplary deterrent from Allah. And Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise.)*
* **Linguistic Analysis & Dual Reading (Section 4.1-E):**
  - **Linguistic Scrutiny (*Al-Kashshaf* - Al-Zamakhshari):**
    - `وَٱلسَّارِقُ وَٱلسَّارِقَةُ`: Commencing with a nominal sentence and the generic/exhaustive definite article (*Al-*) specifies universal legal accountability regardless of gender. Al-Zamakhshari underscores that *Sirqah* (theft) is not merely taking property, but "surreptitiously seizing lawfully guarded property (*Hirz*) without right."
    - `نَكَـٰلًۭا` (*Nakalan*): An exemplary, formidable deterrent designed to preemptively prevent society from repeating the transgression.
    - `عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ`: Concluding with the divine names denoting Sovereign Might and Profound Wisdom indicates the decree is not retributive vengeance, but an enforcement of systemic equilibrium and justice.
  - **Dual Reading Record:**
    - *Reading 1 (Literalist / Zahiri School — Ibn Hazm):* Once the threshold (*Nisab*) is stolen, the amputation is applied unconditionally regardless of economic duress, social crises, or famine; it is an absolute ritualized obligation.
    - *Reading 2 (Purposive / Usuli-Maqasidi School — Four Sunnite Madhhabs Consensus, Ibn Ashur, Ibn al-Qayyim):* Enforcement is constrained by rigorous epistemic filters (*Shubuhat* / reasonable doubts). The Prophetic mandate dictates: *"Avert the prescribed penalties by doubts."* Essential criteria include: protected storage (*Hirz*), stolen value threshold (*Nisab*), absence of acute starvation/duress, and absence of common ownership. Caliph Umar famously suspended this penalty during the Year of Ramadah (Famine) due to systemic systemic necessity.
    - *Preferred Reading:* Reading 2.  
      *Rationale:* In legal philosophy and Maqasid priorities, the preservation of life (*Darurat*) unequivocally takes precedence over property rights (*"Necessities render permissible that which is prohibited"*).

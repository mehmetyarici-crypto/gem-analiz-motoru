### STEP 01 — QURANIC TEXT → CONTEXT → CLASSICAL EXEGESIS (Mandatory + Section 4.1 Dual Reading)
* **Text (Nass):**
  > ٱلرِّجَالُ قَوَّٰمُونَ عَلَى ٱلنِّسَآءِ بِمَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍۢ وَبِمَآ أَنفَقُوا۟ مِنْ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ ۚ فَٱلصَّـٰلِحَـٰتُ قَـٰنِتَـٰتٌ حَـٰفِظَـٰتٌۭ لِّلْغَيْبِ بِمَا حَفِظَ ٱللَّهُ ۚ وَٱلَّـٰتِى تَخَافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَٱهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِى ٱلْمَضَاجِعِ وَٱضْرِبُوهُنَّ ۖ فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا۟ عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيًّۭا كَبِيرًۭا
  *(Men are qawwamun [caretakers, guardians, protectors] over women because of that [advantage] which Allah has given one over the other and because they spend from their wealth. So righteous women are devoutly obedient, guarding in [the husband's] absence what Allah would have them guard. But those from whom you fear nushuz [arrogant rebellion/breach of marital harmony], admonish them [first]; [next], leave them in beds; and [lastly], strike them [symbolically/preventively]. But if they cooperate with you, seek no means against them. Indeed, Allah is ever Exalted and Great.)*
* **Linguistic Analysis & Dual Reading (Section 4.1-E):**
  - **Linguistic Scrutiny (*Al-Kashshaf* - Al-Zamakhshari):**
    - `قَوَّٰمُونَ` (*Qawwamun*): Intensive nominal form (*fa‘‘al* pattern); does not denote "master, owner, or tyrant," but rather "one who upholds an affair, watches over, protects, and manages (*al-qiyam bi'l-amr wa'l-himayah*)." Al-Zamakhshari underlines that *Qawwamiyyah* is not an arbitrary privilege; it is bound by two reciprocal conditions: (1) Divine functional distribution (*bima faddala Allahu ba‘dahum ‘ala ba‘d* — socio-physical functional responsibilities), and (2) Financial expenditure (*wa bima anfaqu min amwalihim* — economic obligations). When the expenditure condition ceases, Qawwamiyyah diminishes accordingly.
    - `نُشُوز` (*Nushuz*): Lexically derived from "rising up, elevating into a mound"; denotes conscious breach of the shared moral and legal marital covenant, threatening familial collapse.
    - `وَٱضْرِبُوهُنَّ` (*Wadribuhunna*): Al-Zamakhshari emphasizes that this sequence is strictly graduated (*tadrij*): first verbal admonition and dialogue (*wa‘z*), then psychological/emotional withdrawal in separate beds (*hijr*), and only as a restricted last resort, symbolic non-injurious intervention before external arbitration.
  - **Dual Reading Record:**
    - *Reading 1 (Traditional Literalist / Juristic School):* The verb *darb* signifies light physical disciplining (without causing injury, bruising, or facial marks, often allegorized with a miswak twig); the husband possesses disciplinary authority (*ta'dib*) as the head of the household.
    - *Reading 2 (Purposive / Investigatory Maqasidi School — Ata ibn Abi Rabah, Ibn Ashur, Contemporary Jurists):*
      - Successor Jurist Ata ibn Abi Rabah: *"A husband shall never strike his wife; the dispensation of striking in this verse is restricted and disapproved by the Prophetic Sunnah (the Prophet never struck any woman or servant during his lifetime)."*
      - Ibn Ashur: The governing authority (*wali al-amr*) possesses the legitimate power to prohibit (*ta'zir*) any physical striking within marriage to curb domestic violence, because the primary intent of the divine text is family reformation (*islah*), whereas domestic violence produces destruction (*ifsad*).
      - *Preferred Reading:* Reading 2.  
        *Rationale:* Grounded in the foundational, explicit Quranic maxims of "affection and mercy" (Ar-Rum 30:21) and "living with them in equity and benevolence" (An-Nisa 4:19), legitimizing domestic violence represents a direct systemic contradiction.

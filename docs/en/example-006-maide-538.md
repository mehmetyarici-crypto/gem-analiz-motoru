## STEP 01 — QURANIC TEXT → CONTEXT → CLASSICAL TAFSĪR (Mandatory + Section 4.1 Dual Reading)

**Wording:**

> وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ فَاقْطَعُوا أَيْدِيَهُمَا جَزَاءً بِمَا كَسَبَا نَكَالًا مِنَ اللَّهِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ

**Literal Analysis and Dual Reading (Section 4.1-E):**

- **Zamakhsharī's Analysis (*al-Kashshāf*):**
  - `وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ`: The fact that it begins with a nominal sentence and that the *alif-lām* indicates genus/participation points to the nature of the crime and to the legal responsibility of the parties without regard to gender distinction. Zamakhsharī emphasizes that the definition of *sirkat* (theft) is not an ordinary act of taking; it is "to take secretly and unjustly a protected (*ḥirz*) property belonging to another."
  - `نَكَالًا`: It means a severe sanction that gives a lesson, deterring others from the same crime and preventing them.
  - `عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ`: It is not vengeful; it concludes with divine names bearing *ʿizz* (the power to apply the ruling) and *ḥikma* (justice and the balance of the social scale).

- **Dual Reading Note:**
  - **Reading 1 (Strict Formalist / Ẓāhirī School — Ibn Ḥazm):** In every act of theft reaching the minimum threshold (*niṣāb*), the hand is cut without regard to conditions, hunger, or social situation; the penalty is an absolute act of worship.
  - **Reading 2 (Uṣūlī / Maqāṣidī School — Consensus of the Four Schools, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ibn al-Qayyim):** The application of this penalty is bound to very strict legal conditions (absence of doubts — *shubuhāt*). The Prophet's rule *"Drop the ḥudūd by doubts"* is fundamental. *Ḥirz* (secure place), *niṣāb*, absence of hunger/necessity, and the property not being public property or a debt are conditions. Indeed, ʿUmar suspended this *ḥadd* in the "Year of Ramāda (Famine)" due to the doubt of hunger.
  - **Preferred:** Reading 2. **Reason:** By the principles of *maqāṣid* and criminal law, the right to survival of a hungry or desperate person (*ḍarūra*) takes precedence over the right of property (the rule *"Necessities make the forbidden permissible"*).

## STEP 12 — COUNTER-READING + RED TEAM + FALSIFIABILITY (Mandatory + v5.1.3)

- **Line 1 (Literalist / Formalist Objection):** "The hand-cutting penalty is Allah's explicit command. To suspend or lighten this penalty by invoking famine, social justice, or *ḥirz* conditions is to compromise the religion."
  - *Response:* The second caliph ʿUmar himself suspended the *ḥadd* in the famine year by applying not the *naṣṣ* but the rule of its application condition, "absence of necessity and doubt." As Shāṭibī states, applying the *ḥadd* before its conditions are met is not justice but murder.
- **Line 2 (Secular Criminological Objection):** "Heavy penalties do not prevent crime; the real thing is education; al-Māʾida 5:38 is out of date."
  - *Response:* Since Beccaria, penal theories have emphasized the "certainty and deterrence" of punishment. If the economic return of a crime exceeds its cost, the rational actor continues to commit it (the case of white-collar crimes). al-Māʾida 5:38 protects public order by making the cost of the crime unbearable for the criminal.
- **Falsifiability Protocol (v5.1.3 Measurable Condition):**
  - *"Data that would overturn this decision: If the international empirical data set (World Bank / Rule of Law Index) documenting that, while heavy deterrent penalties for financial corruption and organized theft are removed and only light supervised release is applied, capital flight in that country has stopped and the property security index has risen, is put forward with a reliability rate ≥ 75%, the decision on the necessity of high deterrence (*nakāl*) falls."*
